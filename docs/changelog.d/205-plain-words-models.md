- **When a model will not start, loopl now says what is wrong and what you can actually do**. The message had been telling people to *tap Repair* — and there is no Repair button in loopl, and never has been: the repair happens by itself, and the only thing to tap is **Try again**. At one point it managed to say *"tap Repair"* and *"Repair failed"* in the same breath. It had also been one message doing duty for three different situations, which meant it was wrong in two of them. Now each says its own thing: if you are offline it tells you it can fix this with a few kilobytes once you are connected, and that the model itself stays on your device; if the fetch failed it says so and Try again is worth a tap; and if the model's files are already correct and it still will not run, it says plainly that nothing here will fix it, that this is in how the model was published rather than anything you did, and suggests another model — without dangling a retry that cannot work.
- **Four other messages about models stopped using the vocabulary of the people who package them**. *Tokenizer files*, *the weights*, *a few KB*, *checkpoints*, *gated repos*, *conversions*, *Metal* and *the quant* all described things in words that only make sense if you build models for a living. The line under the Hugging Face results was the worst of them, because it was also **untrue**: it claimed only *"chat and instruct checkpoints"* were listed, when what is actually left out is models made for speech, transcription and search — a plain base model was always listed. That line is the only place the app explains why a model you expected is missing, so it now says exactly which kinds are left out, and that models needing their owner's permission are among them. The Format picker stopped describing itself and started saying what the choice means, including that opening a GGUF model is where you pick a size: smaller is faster, larger answers better. The names MLX and GGUF stay, because someone choosing between two formats needs them — a word is only jargon when the reader cannot act on it.
