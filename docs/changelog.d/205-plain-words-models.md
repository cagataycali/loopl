- **Four messages about models now explain themselves** (IMPROVE-9). When a model would not start, loopl said its *tokenizer files* were out of date and promised that *the weights* would stay — two words from the trade of packaging models, in the one message that has a **Repair** button under it and therefore the one a person most needs to understand. It now says some of its *small setup files* are out of date, that the fetch is *a few kilobytes*, and that *the model itself stays on your device*. The dead end next to it was worse: when loopl had already fetched fresh files and they came back identical, it ended with *"so the repo itself needs fixing"* — asking the reader to go and repair a repository they do not own. It now says plainly that nothing on this device will fix it, that the fault is in how the model was published, and offers the move that is actually theirs: try another model, and delete this one if you want the space. On the Hugging Face screen, the line under the results claimed *"chat and instruct checkpoints only; gated repos are hidden"* — which was both unreadable and **untrue**, since the filter leaves out models made for speech, transcription and search rather than anything to do with instruction, and a plain base model passes it. That line is the only place the app explains why a model someone expected is missing, so it now says exactly which kinds are left out and that models needing their owner's permission are among them. The Format picker's own caption stopped describing *mlx-community conversions*, *Metal*, *repos* and *the quant*, and started saying what the choice means — including that opening a GGUF model is where you pick a size, smaller being faster and larger answering better. The format names MLX and GGUF stay, because someone choosing between two formats needs them; a word is only jargon when the reader cannot act on it. Both halves of the guard were watched failing on purpose before being trusted, and the retired sentences are now pinned verbatim so they cannot return.
